1. Introduction to Cyber Security

1.1 What is Cyberspace?

Cyberspace is the virtual environment created by computers, networks, software and the Internet. It allows people to communicate, share information, access services and perform online activities.

Examples: Websites, email, online banking, social media and cloud services.

1.2 Computer and Web Technology

Computer technology includes hardware, software and systems used to process and store information. Web technology refers to tools and protocols used to create and access websites and online applications.

  • Hardware: Physical parts of a computer.
  • Software: Programs and applications.
  • Network: Connected computers that exchange information.
  • Web browser: Software used to access websites.
  • Web server: A system that provides website content to users.

1.3 Architecture of Cyberspace

Cyberspace architecture describes how different components work together to provide digital communication.

  • Physical layer: Computers, cables, routers and servers.
  • Logical layer: IP addresses, protocols and software.
  • Application layer: Websites, email, online services and applications.
  • Users: People and organizations using digital services.

1.4 Communication and Web Technology

Digital communication is the exchange of information between devices through wired or wireless networks.

Web technologies use HTML, CSS, JavaScript, HTTP and web servers to deliver websites and online applications.

Examples: Email, video calls, online messaging and file sharing.

1.5 Internet and World Wide Web

The Internet is a worldwide network of interconnected computer networks. It allows devices to exchange information.

The World Wide Web (WWW) is a collection of connected web pages and resources accessed through the Internet.

Difference: The Internet is the network infrastructure, while the WWW is a service that operates on it.

1.6 Internet Infrastructure and Data Transfer

Internet infrastructure consists of physical and logical components that help transfer information across networks.

  • Routers forward data between networks.
  • Switches connect devices within a network.
  • Servers provide online services.
  • Transmission media carry data.
  • Protocols define communication rules.
  • DNS translates domain names into IP addresses.

1.7 Internet Governance and Regulation of Cyberspace

Internet governance refers to the development and application of rules, policies and technical standards that support the operation of the Internet.

It involves governments, technical organizations, private companies and Internet users.

Regulation of cyberspace includes laws and policies related to online safety, privacy, cybercrime and digital activities.

1.8 Meaning of Cyber Security

Cyber Security is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications and data from unauthorized access, attacks, damage or theft.

Main objectives:

  • Confidentiality: Information should be accessible only to authorized people.
  • Integrity: Information should remain accurate and unaltered.
  • Availability: Information and services should be available when required.

These three principles are known as the CIA Triad.

1.9 Issues and Challenges of Cyber Security

  • Increasing cybercrime and online fraud.
  • Data theft and privacy violations.
  • Malware and ransomware attacks.
  • Weak passwords and poor authentication.
  • Unsecured wireless networks.
  • Phishing and social engineering.
  • Rapid development of new technologies.
  • Lack of cyber security awareness.

2. Cybercrime and Cyber Law

2.1 What is Cybercrime?

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities carried out using computers, mobile devices, networks or the Internet.

Examples: Hacking, identity theft, online banking fraud, phishing and spreading malware.

2.2 Classification of Cybercrimes

  • Against individuals: Identity theft, cyberstalking and online harassment.
  • Against property: Data theft, intellectual property theft and ransomware.
  • Against organizations: Network attacks, data breaches and service disruption.
  • Against government: Attacks on government systems and digital infrastructure.

2.3 Cybercrimes Targeting Computers and Mobile Devices

These crimes involve unauthorized access, damage or misuse of computers and mobile devices.

  • Unauthorized access to computer systems.
  • Stealing personal or business information.
  • Installing malicious applications.
  • Taking control of devices without permission.
  • Stealing passwords and login details.

2.4 Cybercrime Against Women and Children

Cybercrime can target women and children through online platforms and digital communication.

  • Cyberstalking and online harassment.
  • Threatening or abusive messages.
  • Impersonation and fake profiles.
  • Sharing private images without consent.
  • Online exploitation and grooming.

Such incidents should be reported to the appropriate authorities and cybercrime reporting channels.

2.5 Financial Frauds

Financial cyber fraud involves stealing money or banking information through digital methods.

  • UPI payment fraud.
  • Fake banking calls and messages.
  • Phishing links and fake websites.
  • Credit and debit card fraud.
  • Fake investment and job offers.

Prevention: Never share OTP, PIN, passwords or confidential banking information. Verify payment requests before approving them.

2.6 Social Engineering Attacks

Social engineering is a technique used by attackers to manipulate people into revealing confidential information or performing unsafe actions.

  • Phishing: Fake messages or websites used to steal information.
  • Pretexting: Creating a false story to gain trust.
  • Baiting: Offering something attractive to trick a victim.
  • Impersonation: Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.

2.7 Malware Attacks

Malware means malicious software designed to damage devices, steal information or gain unauthorized access.

  • Virus: Attaches itself to files and spreads when they are executed.
  • Worm: Spreads automatically between systems.
  • Trojan horse: Appears legitimate but performs harmful actions.
  • Spyware: Secretly collects user information.
  • Ransomware: Locks or encrypts data and demands payment.
  • Adware: Displays unwanted advertisements.

2.8 Zero-Day and Zero-Click Attacks

Zero-day attack: An attack that exploits a software vulnerability before the developer has released a fix or security patch.

Zero-click attack: An attack that can compromise a device without requiring the victim to click a link or open a file.

Both can be dangerous because users may not notice the attack immediately.

2.9 Modus Operandi of Cybercriminals

Modus operandi means the method or procedure used by a criminal to perform an illegal activity.

Common steps used in cyberattacks include:

  • Collecting information about the target.
  • Identifying weaknesses in systems or user behaviour.
  • Using phishing, malware or other attack methods.
  • Gaining unauthorized access.
  • Stealing, changing or destroying information.

2.10 Reporting Cybercrime

Victims should report cybercrime as soon as possible.

  • Preserve messages, screenshots and transaction details.
  • Do not delete important evidence.
  • Contact the relevant bank or payment provider immediately in financial fraud cases.
  • Report cybercrime through the official Indian cybercrime portal.
  • For financial cyber fraud in India, call the national helpline 1930 promptly.
Official portal: https://cybercrime.gov.in
National Cyber Crime Helpline: 1930

2.11 Remedial and Mitigation Measures

Remedial measures help reduce the impact of cyberattacks and prevent future incidents.

  • Change compromised passwords immediately.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Install security updates.
  • Scan devices for malware.
  • Back up important data.
  • Report the incident to the proper authority.
  • Monitor accounts for suspicious activity.

2.12 Information Technology Act, 2000

The Information Technology Act, 2000 is an important Indian law dealing with electronic records, electronic transactions and certain cyber offences.

It provides a legal framework for electronic records and digital activities and includes provisions dealing with unauthorized access, damage to computer resources and other offences.

The Act was amended in 2008 to address changes in technology and cyber-related issues.

2.13 Cyber Security Organizations in India

  • Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In): Handles cyber security incidents and issues security advisories.
  • National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: Provides a channel for reporting cybercrime.
  • National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre (NCIIPC): Works to protect critical information infrastructure.
  • Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C): Supports coordination and response to cybercrime.

2.14 Cybercrime Case Studies

Example 1 – Phishing: A user receives a fake bank message asking them to update account details. The user enters their password on a fake website, allowing the attacker to steal the information.

Example 2 – Ransomware: An employee opens a malicious attachment. The malware encrypts company files and demands payment to restore access.

Lesson: Verify messages, avoid suspicious attachments and maintain secure backups.

3. Social Media Overview and Security

3.1 Introduction to Social Networks

Social networks are online platforms that allow people to communicate, share information, create content and build relationships.

Examples: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube and X.

3.2 Types of Social Media

  • Social networking: Connecting with friends and communities.
  • Media sharing: Sharing photos and videos.
  • Professional networks: Career and professional communication.
  • Discussion platforms: Sharing ideas and asking questions.
  • Blogging platforms: Publishing articles and opinions.

3.3 Social Media Platforms

Social media platforms provide services for communication and content sharing.

  • Instagram – photo and video sharing.
  • Facebook – social networking and communities.
  • LinkedIn – professional networking.
  • YouTube – video publishing and viewing.
  • X – short posts and public discussions.

3.4 Social Media Monitoring

Social media monitoring is the process of observing and analyzing online conversations, comments, mentions and activities related to a person, brand or topic.

It helps organizations understand public opinion, identify problems and respond to users.

3.5 Hashtags and Viral Content

Hashtag: A word or phrase preceded by the # symbol. It helps organize and discover related posts.

Viral content: Content that spreads rapidly across the Internet through sharing, comments and reactions.

Users should verify information before sharing it because false information can also spread quickly.

3.6 Social Media Marketing

Social media marketing is the use of social media platforms to promote products, services, brands or ideas.

  • Creating useful content.
  • Connecting with customers.
  • Running advertisements.
  • Building brand awareness.
  • Analyzing audience engagement.

3.7 Social Media Privacy

Social media privacy refers to controlling how personal information is collected, stored and shared on social platforms.

Privacy protection methods:

  • Use private account settings when appropriate.
  • Avoid sharing sensitive personal information.
  • Review app permissions.
  • Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication.
  • Think before posting photographs or location details.

3.8 Challenges and Opportunities in Online Social Networks

Opportunities:

  • Global communication.
  • Education and knowledge sharing.
  • Business promotion.
  • Professional networking.
  • Community support.

Challenges:

  • Fake news and misinformation.
  • Privacy violations.
  • Cyberbullying.
  • Identity theft.
  • Online scams.

3.9 Pitfalls of Online Social Networks

  • Spending excessive time online.
  • Sharing unverified information.
  • Accepting requests from unknown people.
  • Revealing private information.
  • Becoming a victim of online harassment.
  • Clicking suspicious links.

3.10 Security Issues Related to Social Media

Common social media security problems include fake profiles, account hacking, phishing, malicious links, data leaks and identity theft.

Users should use official applications, enable account security features and avoid suspicious links.

3.11 Reporting Inappropriate Content

Social media platforms provide reporting options to flag abusive, harmful or inappropriate content.

  • Use the platform's report feature.
  • Block accounts that send abusive messages.
  • Save evidence when necessary.
  • Report serious threats or illegal content to the appropriate authorities.

3.12 Laws Regarding Inappropriate Online Content

In India, online content and cyber offences may be governed by applicable provisions of the Information Technology Act, 2000 and other relevant laws.

Users should avoid publishing threats, harassment, defamatory content or private information without permission.

3.13 Best Practices for Social Media

  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Enable two-factor authentication.
  • Keep applications updated.
  • Check privacy settings regularly.
  • Do not share OTPs or passwords.
  • Verify information before sharing.
  • Avoid unknown links and suspicious offers.
  • Report fake accounts and abusive content.

3.14 Social Media Security Case Study

A person receives a message from a fake account claiming to be a friend. The message contains a link asking the person to log in. The fake page collects the login details.

Security lesson: Verify the sender, inspect links carefully and use two-factor authentication.

4. Cyber Security Tools and Technologies

4.1 Antivirus Software

Antivirus software is a security program designed to detect, prevent and remove malicious software from computers.

Functions:

  • Scans files and applications.
  • Detects known malware.
  • Quarantines suspicious files.
  • Provides real-time protection.

Examples: Microsoft Defender, Avast and Bitdefender.

4.2 Firewall

A firewall is a security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic according to defined security rules.

Types:

  • Hardware firewall.
  • Software firewall.
  • Network firewall.
  • Host-based firewall.

Example: A firewall can block unauthorized network connections while allowing approved traffic.

4.3 Intrusion Detection System (IDS)

An Intrusion Detection System monitors network or system activities to identify suspicious behaviour and possible security attacks.

It generates alerts when it detects unusual or unauthorized activity.

4.4 Intrusion Prevention System (IPS)

An Intrusion Prevention System monitors network traffic and can automatically block or prevent detected malicious activities.

Difference: IDS mainly detects and alerts, while IPS can take preventive action to block threats.

4.5 Encryption Tools

Encryption is the process of converting readable information (plaintext) into an unreadable form (ciphertext) using an algorithm and a key.

Decryption converts ciphertext back into readable information.

  • Symmetric encryption: Uses the same key for encryption and decryption.
  • Asymmetric encryption: Uses a public key and a private key.

Encryption helps protect sensitive information during storage and transmission.

4.6 Virtual Private Network (VPN)

A VPN creates an encrypted connection between a device and a VPN service or network. It helps protect data while using networks that may not be trusted.

Uses:

  • Protecting data on public Wi-Fi.
  • Connecting securely to an organization's network.
  • Adding privacy to network communication.

4.7 Authentication Mechanisms

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user or device before granting access.

  • Password authentication: Uses a secret password.
  • OTP: Uses a one-time password.
  • Biometric authentication: Uses fingerprint or facial recognition.
  • Multi-factor authentication: Uses two or more different authentication factors.

4.8 Cyber Hygiene

Cyber hygiene refers to regular practices followed by users to maintain the security and health of digital devices and online accounts.

Important practices:

  • Use strong passwords.
  • Update operating systems and applications.
  • Back up important data.
  • Use antivirus protection.
  • Avoid suspicious websites.
  • Secure online accounts.

4.9 Importance of Strong Passwords

A strong password makes it more difficult for attackers to access an account.

  • Use a long password or passphrase.
  • Combine different character types where appropriate.
  • Avoid names, birthdays and common words.
  • Never reuse the same password everywhere.
  • Use a trusted password manager if needed.

4.10 Regular Software Updates

Software updates fix security weaknesses, correct errors and improve performance.

Outdated software may contain vulnerabilities that attackers can exploit.

Enable automatic updates when possible and install updates from trusted sources.

4.11 Data Backups

A data backup is an additional copy of important files stored separately so that information can be recovered after accidental deletion, hardware failure or a cyberattack.

  • Full backup: Copies all selected data.
  • Incremental backup: Copies data changed since the previous backup.
  • Differential backup: Copies data changed since the last full backup.

4.12 Secure Browsing Habits

  • Use trusted websites.
  • Check the website address carefully.
  • Avoid suspicious downloads.
  • Do not enter passwords on unknown pages.
  • Keep the browser updated.
  • Be careful when using public computers.
  • Log out after using shared devices.

4.13 Network Security Fundamentals

Network security refers to protecting computer networks, connected devices and data from unauthorized access, misuse and attacks.

Main objectives: Protect information, prevent unauthorized access and maintain reliable network services.

4.14 Wired Network Security

Wired network security protects devices connected through physical cables such as Ethernet.

  • Control physical access to network equipment.
  • Use secure router and switch configurations.
  • Restrict unauthorized connections.
  • Monitor network activity.
  • Keep network devices updated.

4.15 Wireless Network Security

Wireless network security protects Wi-Fi networks and connected devices from unauthorized access.

  • Use WPA2 or WPA3 security where supported.
  • Set a strong Wi-Fi password.
  • Change default router administrator credentials.
  • Update router firmware.
  • Disable unnecessary remote administration.
  • Use a guest network for visitors when available.

4.16 Secure Network Configuration

Secure network configuration means setting up network devices and services with appropriate security controls.

  • Change default passwords.
  • Disable unused services and ports.
  • Apply security updates.
  • Use access control rules.
  • Enable encryption.
  • Review firewall settings.

4.17 Network Monitoring

Network monitoring is the continuous observation of network performance, traffic and activities to identify problems and security threats.

It helps administrators detect unusual traffic, unauthorized access and network failures.
