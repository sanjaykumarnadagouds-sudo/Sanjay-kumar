Unit 1 – Introduction to Digital Marketing

1. Introduction to Marketing

Marketing is the process of promoting and selling products or services to customers by understanding their needs and satisfying them.

Objectives of Marketing

  • Identify customer needs.
  • Promote products and services.
  • Increase sales and profit.
  • Build customer relationships.
  • Improve brand awareness.

What is Digital Marketing?

Digital marketing is the promotion of products and services using digital technologies and internet-based platforms such as websites, search engines, social media, email and mobile applications.

Examples

  • Advertising products on Instagram.
  • Sending promotional emails.
  • Promoting a business through Google Search.
  • Publishing videos on YouTube.
  • Running advertisements on websites.

Traditional Marketing vs Digital Marketing

Traditional MarketingDigital Marketing
Uses newspapers, television and radio.Uses websites, social media and email.
Usually has limited audience targeting.Can target specific audiences.
Performance measurement may be difficult.Performance can be measured using analytics.
Often requires higher distribution costs.Can be started with different budget levels.
Communication is often one-way.Allows direct customer interaction.

2. Evolution of Digital Marketing

Digital marketing developed as internet usage, computers, smartphones and online platforms became popular.

Stages of Evolution

  1. Early Internet: Businesses started creating websites to provide information.
  2. Search Engines: Search engines helped users find websites and information.
  3. Email Marketing: Businesses started sending promotional messages through email.
  4. Social Media: Platforms allowed businesses to interact directly with customers.
  5. Mobile Marketing: Smartphones and mobile applications created new marketing opportunities.
  6. Data-Driven Marketing: Analytics and automation helped businesses understand customer behaviour.

Modern Trends

  • Artificial Intelligence in marketing.
  • Influencer marketing.
  • Short-form video marketing.
  • Personalized advertisements.
  • Marketing automation.
  • Voice search and conversational marketing.

3. Importance and Benefits of Digital Marketing

Importance

  • Most customers use the internet to search for products.
  • Businesses can reach customers across different locations.
  • Online platforms help build brand identity.
  • Digital campaigns can be monitored and improved.
  • Customers can communicate with businesses easily.

Benefits

  1. Global Reach: Promotes products to customers worldwide.
  2. Cost Flexibility: Supports campaigns with different budgets.
  3. Targeted Advertising: Reaches selected groups of customers.
  4. Measurable Results: Tracks clicks, views, leads and sales.
  5. Customer Engagement: Allows interaction through comments and messages.
  6. Fast Communication: Shares information quickly.
  7. Brand Awareness: Helps people recognize a business.

4. Digital Marketing Channels and Platforms

A digital marketing channel is a method used by businesses to communicate with customers through digital media.

Important Channels

1. Search Engine Marketing

Promotes websites through search engines using paid advertising and related search marketing methods.

2. Social Media Marketing

Uses social media platforms to promote products, services and brands.

3. Email Marketing

Uses email to communicate with customers, share updates and promote offers.

4. Content Marketing

Creates useful content such as blogs, videos and articles to attract an audience.

5. Mobile Marketing

Promotes products through mobile applications, SMS, mobile websites and mobile advertisements.

6. Affiliate Marketing

Promotes products through partners who earn commissions for successful referrals or sales.

Examples of Platforms

5. Digital Marketing Strategy and Planning

A digital marketing strategy is a planned approach used to achieve business goals through online marketing channels.

Steps to Develop a Strategy

  1. Identify Business Goals: Decide what the business wants to achieve.
  2. Understand Target Audience: Identify customer needs, interests and behaviour.
  3. Analyse Competitors: Study competitors and their marketing activities.
  4. Select Marketing Channels: Choose suitable platforms.
  5. Create Content: Prepare advertisements, videos, posts and articles.
  6. Set Budget: Allocate money to different activities.
  7. Launch Campaign: Publish advertisements and content.
  8. Measure Performance: Study results and improve the strategy.

Example

A coaching academy wants to increase student admissions. It can create a website, publish educational videos, use social media and run local advertisements.

6. Setting Goals and Objectives

Marketing goals are the results that a business wants to achieve through its marketing activities.

Common Digital Marketing Goals

  • Increase website traffic.
  • Generate new leads.
  • Increase product sales.
  • Improve brand awareness.
  • Increase social media followers.
  • Improve customer retention.

SMART Goals

SMART is a method for setting clear and measurable goals.

  • S – Specific: Clearly state the goal.
  • M – Measurable: Track progress using numbers.
  • A – Achievable: Set a realistic target.
  • R – Relevant: Make the goal useful for the business.
  • T – Time-bound: Set a deadline.
Example:
Increase website visitors by 20% within three months.

7. Budgeting and Resource Allocation

Digital marketing budgeting means deciding how much money should be spent on different online marketing activities.

Common Budget Areas

  • Social media advertisements.
  • Search engine advertisements.
  • Website development and hosting.
  • Content creation.
  • Email marketing tools.
  • Analytics and marketing software.

Resource Allocation

Resource allocation means distributing available resources such as money, time, employees and tools among different marketing activities.

Factors Affecting Budget

  • Business size.
  • Marketing objectives.
  • Target audience.
  • Competition.
  • Selected marketing channels.
  • Expected return on investment.

8. Campaign Planning and Execution

A digital marketing campaign is a planned set of online promotional activities designed to achieve a particular goal.

Campaign Planning Steps

  1. Identify campaign objectives.
  2. Select the target audience.
  3. Choose suitable digital platforms.
  4. Prepare campaign content.
  5. Decide the budget and duration.
  6. Schedule and publish advertisements.
  7. Monitor campaign performance.
  8. Make necessary improvements.

Campaign Example

A college may run an online admission campaign using Instagram posts, YouTube videos, a website admission form and targeted advertisements.

9. Monitoring and Adjusting Digital Marketing Campaigns

Monitoring means continuously checking campaign performance. Adjusting means making changes to improve results.

Important Metrics

  • Reach: Number of unique people who see content.
  • Impressions: Total number of times content is displayed.
  • Clicks: Number of times users click a link or advertisement.
  • CTR: Click-through rate.
  • Conversions: Desired actions completed by users.
  • Engagement: Likes, comments, shares and other interactions.

Click-Through Rate Formula

CTR = (Number of Clicks / Number of Impressions) × 100

Example

An advertisement receives 100 clicks from 2,000 impressions.

CTR = (100 / 2000) × 100 = 5%

12. Creating and Optimizing Social Media Profiles

A social media profile represents a person, organization or business on a social media platform.

Steps to Create a Business Profile

  1. Select a suitable social media platform.
  2. Choose a professional business name.
  3. Upload a clear profile picture or logo.
  4. Write a short business description.
  5. Add contact details.
  6. Add website and location information when relevant.
  7. Publish useful and regular content.

Profile Optimization

  • Use relevant keywords in the description.
  • Maintain consistent branding.
  • Use high-quality images.
  • Keep contact information updated.
  • Respond to customer messages.
  • Include a clear call to action.

Types of Content

14. Social Media Advertising

Social media advertising is paid promotion of products, services or content through social media platforms.

Types of Social Media Advertisements

  • Image Ads: Advertisements using pictures.
  • Video Ads: Advertisements using videos.
  • Carousel Ads: Advertisements containing multiple images or cards.
  • Story Ads: Full-screen advertisements displayed in stories.
  • Lead Ads: Advertisements designed to collect customer enquiries.
  • Sponsored Posts: Paid posts shown to selected audiences.

Targeting Options

  • Location.
  • Age group.
  • Interests.
  • Language.
  • Behaviour and engagement.

Advantages

  • Reaches selected audiences.
  • Supports different advertising budgets.
  • Provides campaign performance reports.
  • Can generate enquiries and sales.

15. Social Media Analytics

Social media analytics is the process of collecting and analysing social media data to understand marketing performance.

Important Metrics

  • Followers: People who follow an account.
  • Reach: Unique people who viewed content.
  • Impressions: Total content displays.
  • Engagement Rate: Level of audience interaction.
  • Link Clicks: Number of clicks on links.
  • Conversions: Actions completed by users.

Engagement Rate Formula

Engagement Rate = (Total Engagements / Reach) × 100

Uses

  • Understand audience interests.
  • Identify successful content.
  • Improve posting schedules.
  • Measure campaign performance.

16. Introduction to Email Marketing

Email marketing is a digital marketing method that uses email to communicate with customers, share information and promote products or services.

Objectives

  • Promote products and services.
  • Share news and updates.
  • Build customer relationships.
  • Send special offers.
  • Increase website visits and sales.

Types of Marketing Emails

  • Promotional Emails: Announce discounts and offers.
  • Welcome Emails: Welcome new subscribers.
  • Newsletter Emails: Share regular information.
  • Transactional Emails: Confirm orders or transactions.
  • Reminder Emails: Remind users about important activities.

17. Building an Email List

An email list is a collection of email addresses belonging to people who have agreed to receive communications from a business.

Methods to Build an Email List

  • Website subscription forms.
  • Newsletter registration.
  • Event registration.
  • Free downloadable resources.
  • Customer enquiry forms.
  • Permission-based sign-up campaigns.

Best Practices

  • Collect consent before sending marketing emails.
  • Provide clear information about subscriptions.
  • Keep customer information secure.
  • Provide an unsubscribe option.
  • Do not purchase or use unauthorized email lists.

18. Creating Effective Email Campaigns

An email campaign is a planned series of emails sent to a target audience to achieve a marketing objective.

Steps

  1. Define the campaign objective.
  2. Select the target audience.
  3. Write an attractive subject line.
  4. Create clear and useful email content.
  5. Add suitable images and links.
  6. Include a call to action.
  7. Test the email on different devices.
  8. Send and measure the campaign.

Elements of an Effective Email

  • Sender name.
  • Subject line.
  • Greeting.
  • Main message.
  • Call-to-action button or link.
  • Contact information.
  • Unsubscribe link.

19. Email Automation and Segmentation

Email Automation

Email automation means sending emails automatically when a specific event or condition occurs.

Examples

  • Automatic welcome emails.
  • Order confirmation emails.
  • Birthday greetings.
  • Subscription reminders.
  • Follow-up emails.

Email Segmentation

Email segmentation means dividing subscribers into smaller groups based on their interests, behaviour or characteristics.

Segmentation Methods

  • Age group.
  • Location.
  • Purchase history.
  • Interests.
  • Customer activity.

Difference

AutomationSegmentation
Sends emails automatically.Divides subscribers into groups.
Based on triggers or schedules.Based on subscriber information.

20. Email Marketing Metrics and Analytics

Email analytics measures the performance of email campaigns.

Important Metrics

  • Delivery Rate: Percentage of emails successfully delivered.
  • Open Rate: Percentage of delivered emails recorded as opened.
  • Click-Through Rate: Percentage of delivered emails that generated clicks, depending on the reporting method used.
  • Bounce Rate: Percentage of emails that could not be delivered.
  • Unsubscribe Rate: Percentage of recipients who unsubscribe.
  • Conversion Rate: Percentage of recipients who complete a desired action.

Open Rate Formula

Open Rate = (Unique Opens / Delivered Emails) × 100

Click-Through Rate Formula

CTR = (Unique Clicks / Delivered Emails) × 100

Importance

  • Measures campaign performance.
  • Identifies successful subject lines.
  • Helps improve email content.
  • Helps understand subscriber behaviour.

Unit 3 – Content Marketing and Mobile Marketing

21. Understanding Content Marketing

Content marketing is a strategy of creating and sharing valuable, relevant and useful content to attract and retain a target audience.

Types of Content

  • Blog articles.
  • Videos.
  • Infographics.
  • Social media posts.
  • Podcasts.
  • E-books.
  • Case studies.
  • Email newsletters.

Benefits

  • Builds brand awareness.
  • Provides useful information.
  • Builds trust with customers.
  • Supports search engine visibility.
  • Attracts potential customers.

22. Content Strategy and Planning

A content strategy is a plan for creating and distributing content to reach specific marketing goals.

Steps in Content Planning

  1. Identify the target audience.
  2. Understand audience needs.
  3. Set content goals.
  4. Select content formats.
  5. Choose suitable platforms.
  6. Create a publishing calendar.
  7. Prepare and review content.
  8. Publish and measure results.

Content Planning Example

A coaching academy can create educational articles, programming tutorials, admission announcements and student success stories.

23. Content Creation and Distribution

Content Creation

Content creation is the process of preparing useful information in different formats for a target audience.

Steps

  • Select a topic.
  • Research the subject.
  • Prepare an outline.
  • Create the content.
  • Check accuracy and quality.
  • Publish the content.

Content Distribution

Content distribution means sharing created content through different channels so that the target audience can access it.

Distribution Channels

  • Company websites.
  • Blogs.
  • Social media platforms.
  • Email newsletters.
  • Video-sharing platforms.
  • Online communities.

24. Content Promotion and Amplification

Content promotion is the process of increasing the visibility of published content. Content amplification uses additional channels and methods to reach a larger audience.

Methods

  • Share content on social media.
  • Send content through newsletters.
  • Use relevant keywords.
  • Collaborate with influencers.
  • Promote useful posts through paid advertising.
  • Share content in relevant online communities.
  • Repurpose articles into videos or infographics.

Benefits

  • Increases content reach.
  • Attracts website visitors.
  • Improves audience engagement.
  • Increases brand awareness.

25. Content Marketing Metrics and Analytics

Content marketing analytics measures how audiences interact with published content.

Important Metrics

  • Page Views: Number of times a page is viewed.
  • Unique Visitors: Number of distinct visitors.
  • Time on Page: Time spent viewing a page.
  • Engagement: Audience interactions with content.
  • Shares: Number of times content is shared.
  • Conversions: Desired actions completed after viewing content.

Why Analytics Is Important

  • Identifies popular topics.
  • Measures audience interest.
  • Improves content quality.
  • Helps plan future content.

26. Introduction to Mobile Marketing

Mobile marketing is the promotion of products and services through mobile devices such as smartphones and tablets.

Mobile Marketing Channels

  • SMS marketing.
  • Mobile applications.
  • Mobile websites.
  • Mobile advertisements.
  • Push notifications.
  • Location-based messages.

Advantages

  • Reaches customers through devices they use regularly.
  • Supports location-based promotion.
  • Allows quick communication.
  • Provides interactive experiences.
  • Can measure campaign performance.

27. Mobile Advertising Strategies

Mobile advertising is the promotion of products and services through advertisements displayed on mobile devices.

Types

  • Banner Ads: Small advertisements displayed within applications or websites.
  • Video Ads: Promotional videos shown on mobile platforms.
  • Native Ads: Advertisements designed to match the style of the platform.
  • In-App Ads: Advertisements displayed inside mobile applications.
  • Search Ads: Advertisements displayed in mobile search results.

Effective Strategies

  • Use mobile-friendly designs.
  • Keep messages short and clear.
  • Use suitable images and videos.
  • Make landing pages load quickly.
  • Provide clear calls to action.
  • Respect user privacy and permissions.

28. Mobile App Marketing

Mobile app marketing is the process of promoting a mobile application to attract users, increase downloads and encourage continued use.

Important Activities

  • App Store Optimization (ASO).
  • App launch campaigns.
  • Social media promotion.
  • Referral campaigns.
  • Push notifications.
  • User engagement campaigns.

App Store Optimization

ASO improves the visibility of an application in app stores.

ASO Techniques

  • Choose a suitable app name.
  • Write a clear app description.
  • Use relevant keywords.
  • Upload attractive screenshots.
  • Maintain good app quality.
  • Encourage genuine user reviews.

29. Location-Based Marketing

Location-based marketing delivers relevant advertisements or messages based on a user's geographical location, subject to appropriate permissions and privacy requirements.

Technologies Used

  • GPS.
  • Wi-Fi positioning.
  • Bluetooth beacons.
  • Geofencing.
  • Mobile location services.

Examples

  • A restaurant displaying offers to nearby customers.
  • A shopping centre sending an offer to visitors who opted in.
  • A local business promoting services in a selected area.

Advantages

  • Reaches relevant local audiences.
  • Supports location-specific offers.
  • Improves local business promotion.

Privacy Considerations

Businesses should obtain appropriate consent, explain location use and protect customer information.

30. Mobile Marketing Analytics

Mobile marketing analytics is the process of measuring and analysing the performance of mobile marketing campaigns and applications.

Important Metrics

  • App downloads.
  • Active users.
  • App retention rate.
  • Click-through rate.
  • Conversion rate.
  • Uninstall rate.
  • In-app purchases.
  • Campaign engagement.

Importance

  • Measures mobile campaign success.
  • Identifies user behaviour.
  • Improves application experience.
  • Helps optimize advertisements.
  • Supports better marketing decisions.
